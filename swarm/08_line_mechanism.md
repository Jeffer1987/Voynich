# 08  Mechanism of the line-final -m and line-initial effects (Wave 4A)

Scripts (`python -I`, args `zl3b|rf1b <n_shuffles>`; 300 used): `scripts/m4_common.py` (own parser that keeps the `.`/`,` separators and the `<$>` paragraph-end marker; ZL3b reproduces 3310 continuation lines / 27,286 words of 05), `m4a.py` (tests 1-3), `m4b.py` (1c, 4, 5, ZL-vs-RF alignment), `m4c.py` (per-line state). Raw outputs: `scripts/_m4*_*.txt`. Same line set as 05 (P-lines, not paragraph-first, >=3 words). RF1b has no `<$>` marker, so paragraph-end tests are ZL only; RF1b replicates everything else (values in brackets).

## Results by test
**1. Paired stem alternation (stem = word minus last glyph).**
- Within-stem position-label permutation, 563 stems seen both mid and line-final (2628 final tokens): final minus mid share of last glyph: m +0.141 (z +27), l -0.076 (z -11), r -0.099 (z -13), o -0.028 (z -7), g +0.020, y +0.023, n +0.011. So -m replaces -l and -r (about equally) and, less, -o. [RF1b same pattern.]
- Stems of final-m words occur mid-line as -l (42%) and -r (46%), almost never -y (0.8% vs 42% for stems of other final words), -n 4% (vs 18%). 92% / 94% of final-m tokens have their stem attested mid-line as -l / -r (vs 53% / 49% for other final tokens; z 28-36). Stem attested at all: 82% vs 79%, i.e. no lexical rarity.
- Among words ending l/r/m: m-share is 44% at line end vs 2.9% mid-line [41% vs 2.4%]. By preceding glyph: -am 54% (vs 5% mid), -om 24% (vs 1.3%), -iim ~56%. So it is chiefly the -al/-ar -> -am substitution.
- Not fully deterministic: per-stem m-share varies more than binomial (variance 0.055 vs 0.023, z 5.2 [4.9]); stems range from never to always -m at line end.
- Gradient: -m is 18.9% at the last word, 1.8% second-last, 0.7% third [19.0 / 1.1 / 0.6]: the substitution is confined to the last word.

**2. Margin fill vs wrapped/paragraph-final lines.**
- Raw fill effect (prefix chars) is real (logit b=0.44/sd, z 8.7), but it is carried by words per line: with n words in the model the char-length term is b=0.22 (z 1.6) [0.08, z 0.6]; with folio fixed effects b_pre=0.14 (z 0.9) [0.06, z 0.4], b_n=0.38 (z 2.4) [0.41, z 2.8]. Within n-word strata the -m rate does not rise with characters (|r| <= 0.07, z <= 2.1); at fixed n, longer words do not raise it (z 1.0). Folio-relative fill (distance to the longest line of the page) adds nothing (z 0.5). -m rate by words per line: 3-4 w 8.5%, 7-8 w 12.9%, 11-13 w 25.6% [9.1 / 13.8 / 26.3].
- Paragraph-final lines (n=686, short: 6.3 words, 30 chars vs 8.8 / 46 for wrapped lines): -m 9.3% (-g 1.0%) vs 16.9% for wrapped. Matched on words and prefix chars (+-3): -m 9.2% vs 11.1% expected (z -1.6); m|g 10.2% vs 13.7% (z -2.7); with folio fixed effects para-end flag b=-0.67 (z -3.3). So -m is present at ~9% on short lines that never reach the margin (6x to 9x the mid-line rate), only mildly reduced (~20-30%) relative to matched wrapped lines.

**3. Word-break artifact.**
- Uncertain gap `,` before the last word: 12.0% of lines vs 7.5% of mid gaps [3.1 vs 1.9%] (mild edge enrichment, last words then short: 3.6 vs 5.0 chars), but -m rate is the same: 15.1% with `,` vs 15.4% with `.` [16.5 vs 14.8].
- Lines without any uncertain gap in the last two gaps: 15.5% vs 14.6% with. Clean subset (certain gaps on both sides of every word counted): final 15.4%, mid 0.98%, ratio 15.7x (all lines 15.8x) [17.2x].
- ZL vs RF lines with identical letters but different word splits (n=70, last word differs): -m rate 12.9% in both transcriptions vs 12.9% when identical. Standalone words "m"/"am" are rare (m: 1 mid, 4 final; am 11 vs 44), so -m is not a detached fragment.
- No sign of merge/split causing the effect.

**4. Line-initial.**
- Strip test (remainder after removing the first glyph is an attested mid-line word type, vs length-matched mid-line words with the same first glyph): y z +0.5 [+3.2], d +3.9 [+3.7], t +3.3 [+3.2], p +2.5 [-3.7], s +1.5 [+3.2], controls o, q, k, l ~0. Attestation is 82-94% everywhere, with at most +3-5 points over mid-line: y/d/t are words-with-prefix also in mid-line; at line start the evidence for an extra prefix is weak and not stable (y, p, s disagree between transcriptions).
- Length (Vogt): first words are +0.34 (y), +0.33 (d), +0.47 (t) glyphs longer than mid-line words with the same first glyph (z 3-5, both transcriptions), but o, q, k, l +-0.1 (n.s.). So the lengthening is specific to the y/d/t/(s) class, not a general first-word effect; it is partly more than one glyph's worth of prefix (+0.3 after conditioning on the first glyph).
- After y/d the remainder starts with a- (28% vs 6%) / k, t more than expected (p ~ 0): the prefix attaches to a restricted set of stems.
- Co-variation: per folio (n=100 pages >=12 lines) rho(final -m, initial y/d) = 0.42 [0.42], but a (section,hand)-stratified permutation null already gives 0.23 [0.16]: z 2.5 [3.3]. Per line there is no coupling: y/d start given same-line final m 39.4% vs 35.2% (z -0.2 [-1.1]); next-line y/d start after final m 39.1% vs 35.5% (z -1.0 [+1.3]).

**5. Within-section/hand cells (>=150 lines) and per-line state.** Ratio of final-m to mid-line rate: Herbal/hand1 6x, Stars/hand3 24x, Balneo/hand2 330x (mid 0.02%), Herbal/hand2 15x, Pharma 12x, Text/hand2 28x [RF]. The effect and the words-per-line dependence hold inside cells (r(-m, n words) = 0.06-0.19; significant in Stars/hand3, z 5.8 [5.3], Herbal/hand1 z 2.3 [3.1]; not in the small cells). Initial y/d ratio 1.8x-7.7x per cell. Page-level overdispersion of final-m given section and hand: chi2/df = 1.45 [1.50] (pages differ in a way not explained by line-level independence). P(final m | another -m already in the line) = 30% vs 14.5% (z 2.2 [2.3], n=171): mild line-level clustering. Share of -l/-r words earlier in the line does not predict it (z 1.1). Line-to-line persistence of final-m: r ~0.04-0.06, and my within-folio permutation null behaves unreliably (z -2.7 / -3.1 with the wrong sign), so persistence beyond page level is inconclusive.

## Verdicts
| mechanism | verdict | reason |
|---|---|---|
| (i) scribal end-form / abbreviation convention (-m as positional allograph of -l/-r, mainly -ar/-al -> -am) | **supported** (best fit) | Position-conditioned replacement of l/r by m on the same stems, restricted to the last word (no gradient), present on short, non-margin lines, in every cell and in both transcriptions; scribe/page variation (Currier B, hand 3 higher) fits a habit with hand dependence. Not deterministic (stem-level overdispersion). |
| (ii) margin/space filling | **weakened** (largely refuted for crowding) | Character fill and folio-relative fill add nothing once words per line and folio are controlled; -m is 9% on paragraph-final lines that stop far from the margin. A residual dependence on words per line (8.5% -> 25%) and a modest wrapped-vs-paragraph-end difference remain, so a weak "end of a full line" component cannot be excluded. |
| (iii) generator with per-line state | **weakened, not refuted** | Line-level clustering (30% vs 14.5%), words-per-line dependence and page-level overdispersion (1.5x) are compatible with a state that varies per line/page, and no test here separates "scribe style per page" from "generator state". But -m is restricted to the very last word, and the line-start slot (y/d/t) is uncoupled from the line-end slot at line level (z ~0) while only page-level covariance exists (rho 0.42, mostly explained by section/hand). No evidence for a single line state driving both ends. |
| (iv) transcription word-break artifact | **refuted** for -m | Effect unchanged with/without uncertain gaps (15.1 vs 15.4%, ratio 15.7x on the clean subset), unchanged where ZL and RF split the last word differently (12.9% both), replicates in an independent transcription, standalone m/am are rare. Uncertain gaps are mildly enriched at line ends (12% vs 7.5%) but irrelevant to -m. Line-initial: the y/d/t words are real words, not fragments, but strip-test results differ across transcriptions, so the initial "prefix" reading is weak. |

## Caveats
- Stems are defined by the final glyph only (EVA parsed with ch/sh/cXh as units; -iin, -iir endings count their last glyph); a different parsing (e.g. -iin as unit) was not run here.
- ZL and RF are not independent of a common ancestry of readings (both EVA); replication is a check on word-breaks and glyph readings, not on the manuscript. Permutation p are limited to 1/301; z is given.
- Within-folio permutation for line-to-line autocorrelation is unreliable here (sign inconsistent); do not interpret it.
- Distinguishing (i) from (iii) needs the Wave-next gibberish-null calibration (07 test 3: does a scribe copying a generator output produce this edge effect?) and a per-scribe analysis of page-level -m variation.
